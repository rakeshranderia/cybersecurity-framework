# Third-Party & Supply Chain Risk

## Overview

Modern organisations depend on a growing ecosystem of technology providers, SaaS platforms, managed services and strategic suppliers.

Third-party risk management should therefore form part of the organisation's broader cybersecurity and operational risk framework.

---

## Risk Assessment Lifecycle

A practical third-party security assessment should follow a structured lifecycle:

**Identify → Assess → Treat → Monitor → Review → Exit**

### Identify

Understand:

- What service is being provided
- What information is involved
- What systems are accessed
- Business criticality
- Regulatory requirements
- Dependencies and subcontractors

### Assess

Evaluate:

- Information security controls
- Privacy requirements
- Access management
- Data protection
- Business continuity
- Incident response
- Vulnerability management
- Security certifications and assurance
- Subcontractor dependencies

### Treat

Determine appropriate risk treatments, such as:

- Additional contractual requirements
- Compensating controls
- Additional monitoring
- Risk acceptance
- Alternative suppliers
- Service restrictions

### Monitor

Supplier risk should be monitored throughout the relationship rather than only at onboarding.

Monitoring may include:

- Annual security assessments
- Assurance reports
- Security incidents
- Material changes
- Control changes
- Service performance
- External security intelligence

### Exit

Exit planning should consider:

- Data return
- Data destruction
- Access removal
- Credential revocation
- Transition to replacement providers
- Knowledge transfer
- Continuity of critical services

---

## Risk-Based Tiering

Not every supplier requires the same level of assessment.

A practical model is:

| Tier | Example | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Core business platform | Detailed assessment + ongoing monitoring |
| **High** | Sensitive data / privileged access | Detailed security assessment |
| **Medium** | Business application | Standard assessment |
| **Low** | Low-risk service | Proportionate due diligence |

---

## Executive Perspective

Third-party risk should be considered as an extension of organisational risk.

The key question is not:

> "Does the supplier have a security certificate?"

It is:

> **"Does the supplier's control environment reduce the risks associated with the service we are purchasing to an acceptable level?"**
