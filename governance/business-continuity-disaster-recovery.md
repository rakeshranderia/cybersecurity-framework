# Cybersecurity Metrics & Executive Reporting

## Overview

Cybersecurity reporting should provide executives and Boards with meaningful visibility of security risk, control effectiveness and improvement.

Technical metrics should be translated into business outcomes wherever possible.

---

## Metric Categories

### Risk

Examples:

- Number of material cybersecurity risks
- Residual risk
- Risk trend
- High-risk exceptions
- Overdue remediation

### Vulnerability Management

Examples:

- Critical vulnerabilities
- Vulnerabilities past remediation target
- Patch compliance
- Average remediation time

### Identity

Examples:

- MFA coverage
- Privileged accounts
- Dormant accounts
- Access review completion

### Security Operations

Examples:

- Security incidents
- Mean Time to Detect
- Mean Time to Respond
- High-severity alerts
- Incident trends

### Resilience

Examples:

- Backup success rate
- Recovery test results
- Recovery objective achievement
- Disaster recovery findings

### Third-Party Risk

Examples:

- Critical suppliers assessed
- Overdue supplier assessments
- High-risk suppliers
- Supplier security incidents

---

## Executive Dashboard

A useful executive dashboard should answer:

1. **What is our current security posture?**
2. **What has changed since the previous reporting period?**
3. **What are our most significant risks?**
4. **Are controls operating effectively?**
5. **Where are we investing?**
6. **What decisions or support are required?**

---

## Avoiding Vanity Metrics

Metrics should support decision-making.

For example:

> "We blocked 10 million threats"

may sound impressive but provides limited insight into organisational risk.

A more useful measure might be:

> "Critical security incidents decreased by 35% while critical vulnerability remediation improved from 72% to 96%."

The objective is to demonstrate **risk reduction and control effectiveness**, not simply activity.

---

## Executive Perspective

Effective cybersecurity reporting converts technical information into:

**Risk → Impact → Trend → Action → Decision**

This enables executives and Boards to understand cybersecurity as a business risk rather than simply a technology issue.
