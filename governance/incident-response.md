# Cybersecurity Incident Response

## Overview

Incident response provides a structured approach for identifying, containing, managing and recovering from cybersecurity incidents.

An effective response capability combines technology, people, processes, communications and decision-making.

---

## Incident Lifecycle

A practical incident lifecycle is:

**Prepare → Detect → Assess → Contain → Eradicate → Recover → Learn**

---

## 1. Prepare

Preparation should include:

- Incident response plan
- Roles and responsibilities
- Contact lists
- Escalation procedures
- Technical capabilities
- Backup and recovery
- Communication plans
- Tabletop exercises

---

## 2. Detect

Security events may be identified through:

- SIEM
- EDR / XDR
- User reports
- Security monitoring
- Threat intelligence
- Third-party notifications
- External reporting

---

## 3. Assess

Determine:

- What happened?
- What systems are affected?
- What information may be affected?
- Is the incident ongoing?
- What is the business impact?
- Are regulatory notifications required?
- Does the incident require executive escalation?

---

## 4. Contain

Containment aims to prevent further damage.

Actions may include:

- Isolating systems
- Disabling accounts
- Blocking malicious activity
- Restricting network access
- Preserving evidence

---

## 5. Eradicate

Remove the underlying cause of the incident.

This may include:

- Removing malware
- Patching vulnerabilities
- Resetting credentials
- Removing persistence mechanisms
- Rebuilding compromised systems

---

## 6. Recover

Restore affected services safely.

Recovery should include:

- System restoration
- Validation
- Increased monitoring
- Business verification
- Stakeholder communication

---

## 7. Learn

Every significant incident should result in lessons learned.

Consider:

- What happened?
- Why did it happen?
- Which controls worked?
- Which controls failed?
- What should change?
- What residual risk remains?

---

## Executive Decision-Making

During a major incident, executives need clear information rather than technical detail.

A useful executive incident update should communicate:

**What happened → Business impact → Current risk → Actions underway → Decisions required → Expected next steps**

---

## Executive Perspective

Incident response is ultimately a business capability.

The technology team may lead the technical response, but major incidents require coordination across:

**Technology + Security + Legal + Risk + Communications + Operations + Executive Leadership**
