# SOC 2

## Overview

SOC 2 is an assurance framework focused on controls relevant to security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy.

It is particularly relevant when assessing technology service providers and SaaS organisations.

---

## Trust Services Criteria

SOC 2 uses five Trust Services Criteria:

| Criteria | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Security** | Protection against unauthorised access and system threats |
| **Availability** | Systems are available for operation and use |
| **Processing Integrity** | Processing is complete, valid, accurate and timely |
| **Confidentiality** | Confidential information is protected |
| **Privacy** | Personal information is collected, used, retained and disposed of appropriately |

Security is the common criteria for all SOC 2 examinations, while the other criteria are included where relevant to the engagement.

---

## Executive Application

SOC 2 can provide useful assurance when evaluating technology suppliers.

Key areas to consider include:

- Scope of the report
- Services covered
- Control environment
- Exceptions identified
- Complementary user entity controls
- Subservice organisations
- Report period
- Type I vs Type II assurance

---

## Type I vs Type II

### Type I

Provides assurance that controls are suitably designed at a specific point in time.

### Type II

Provides assurance over the design and operating effectiveness of controls over a defined period.

For supplier risk management, Type II reports generally provide greater insight into whether controls have operated consistently.

---

## Executive Perspective

SOC 2 should be considered as one input into third-party risk assessment rather than automatically treated as evidence that a supplier is "secure".

The organisation should assess whether:

**The scope + controls + exceptions + service provided = acceptable residual risk**

---

## Key Questions for Supplier Assessment

- Is the relevant service included in the report?
- What period does the report cover?
- Were control exceptions identified?
- Are there complementary controls required from our organisation?
- Are critical subservice providers included?
- Does the report address the risks relevant to our use case?
